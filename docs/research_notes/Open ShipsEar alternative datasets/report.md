# UniqueShip is the only open ShipsEar fallback

No dataset found is a free, openly licensed, reliably hosted drop-in for ShipsEar. One comes close as a fallback and second source: **UniqueShip**, which is 2,460 h of Ocean Networks Canada (ONC) Strait of Georgia hydrophone audio from 4,218 vessels, AIS-labelled, with vessel-disjoint splits, stated as **CC-BY 4.0**, and downloadable without a login in subsets of **26 GB or 92 GB** ([uniqueshipdata.org](https://uniqueshipdata.org), [arXiv 2609.13659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13659)). Its labels are cargo, tanker, passenger, tug and background, so it cannot reproduce ShipsEar's classes A-E: the small-craft class B has no counterpart, and class D (ocean liners, ro-ro) has only a loose one. The licence statement is the weak point, because it was read on a project website through a page summariser and not on a DOI-registered repository, and the files sit on Google Drive. Every other candidate fails one of the gates. DeepShip, Oceanship and QiandaoEar22 have no stated licence and gated or fragile hosting (author email, Baidu Pan, a subscriber-only IEEE DataPort page); the archives hold audio without vessel-type labels; and DeuteroNoise and the Belgian VLIZ set are promising supplements that are small, unconfirmed or both. My recommendation is to keep ShipsEar as the v1.0 primary, as the PRD says, and to start the UniqueShip 26 GB download this week as an insurance policy, a P2 source and a source of redistributable demo clips. If the ShipsEar maintainers have not supplied the full data by the end of Week 1 (Oct 14), switch the primary to UniqueShip with a new four-vessel-class-plus-background scope, which needs a PRD amendment.

## Only two candidates meet all four gates, and one is still unconfirmed

The gates are real field audio with vessel-type labels, an open licence, no login or manual request, and hosting that survives a year of reproduction attempts. The table summarises each candidate. The "Status" column says what the licence claim rests on, because several rest on a summary and not on a primary page.

The table's headline is that licence cleanliness and rigor point to the same place. UniqueShip was built because earlier ONC-derived benchmarks leak: its authors report that random partitioning in two prominent datasets inflates test accuracy by **10 to 48 points**, and on DeepShip with a Swin model vessel-disjoint splits cost about 13-14 F1 points ([arXiv 2609.13659](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13659)). That fits the PRD's reason for existing. UniqueShip also shares its upstream with DeepShip, VTUAD and Oceanship, which all come from the same ONC Strait of Georgia hydrophones ([Oceanship paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.02099)), so those four are not independent of each other. Only the Belgian set, DeuteroNoise and QiandaoEar22 add a different site and instrument. The VTUAD name is also ambiguous: a 2026 preprint describes a 525-clip, 56 h, 12-category set with a CC-BY claim, which I could not verify because the preprint page returned HTTP 403 ([preprints.org 202609.0617](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202609.0617)). It is a different object from the subscriber-only DataPort set.

The ranking for this project follows. UniqueShip is first. The VLIZ set is second as an independent test source, but it must be opened and checked before anything depends on it. DeuteroNoise is third because it is the only candidate with workboats and leisure craft, though its release is unconfirmed. DeepShip is fourth, because a good dataset with no licence and email-gated access fails the "free, open, reliably hosted" test even though its metafiles allow vessel grouping. Oceanship is fifth, QiandaoEar22 sixth, and the subscriber-only VTUAD is excluded.

## Archives hold open audio but not the labels

The upstream archives are open but unlabelled for vessel type. ONC is the root of the labelled sets: ONC's hydrophone audio can be downloaded through an Oceans 3.0 account and API, and the dataset authors state the data are CC-BY 4.0, but I could not find that licence on an ONC-hosted page. ONC's Legal Notices page restricts reuse of its website content without permission and says nothing about data licensing ([ONC Legal Notices](https://www.oceannetworks.ca/legal-notices/)). The only wording I have comes from a summary of the ONC system paper, which says "most data" are CC-BY 4.0, with some datasets restricted ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.806452/full)). Building your own AIS-joined set from raw ONC is a multi-week job (AIS retrieval, spatiotemporal join, closest-approach filtering) and does not fit the Oct 31 deadline.

NOAA's passive acoustic archive is open and cloud-hosted, and the SanctSound vessel products are binary ship-present events: one Olympic Coast product holds 1,805 events from 11 Jul to 30 Oct 2019, flagged vessel or ambient with no AIS or type ([ERDDAP OC02_02](https://coastwatch.pfeg.noaa.gov/erddap/info/noaaSanctSound_OC02_02_ships/index.html), [NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/passive-acoustic-data)). That helps find ambient windows but gives no classes. MBARI Pacific Sound is CC BY 4.0 on the AWS Open Data registry at about 2 TB per month, but has no vessel labels and sits at 900 m depth ([MBARI](https://www.mbari.org/data/passive-acoustic-data/), [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/pacific-sound)). OOI states no specific licence beyond acknowledgement and delivers hydrophone data as miniSEED ([OOI forum](https://discourse.oceanobservatories.org/t/license-type-for-mseed-hydrophone-recordings/753)). Orcasound is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, whose non-commercial and share-alike terms conflict with an open or commercial deliverable ([AWS Orcasound](https://registry.opendata.aws/orcasound)). The Australian IMOS release is CC BY 4.0 but contains source-level spectra as CSV, not audio ([Research Data Australia](https://researchdata.edu.au/underwater-noise-signatures-australian-waters/1712034)). None of these is a v1.0 classification dataset. Their value is as a later source of ambient clips; this report did not review EMODnet, JOMOPANS, Watkins or US Navy releases, so absence from this list is not a negative finding for them.

## Mapping to classes A-E is partial, so the taxonomy must change if the data does

ShipsEar's five classes are A (fishing boats, trawlers, mussel boats, tugboats, dredgers), B (motorboats, pilot boats, sailboats), C (passenger ferries), D (ocean liners, ro-ro) and E (noise) ([ShipsEar paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apacoust.2016.06.008)). The ONC-derived sets are organised by AIS type, so the mapping is my inference and has not been validated by any published crosswalk. Tug maps to part of A, and passenger to C, though the ONC passenger class is mostly large ferries in a ferry corridor. Cargo and tanker are the nearest to D, as large merchant vessels, but ro-ro and ocean liners are not the same thing. Class B has no counterpart in the 5-class UniqueShip subset or in DeepShip; the 12-class UniqueShip class list was not retrieved and may include leisure craft, which is the first thing to read in its Fig. 2 or on the download page. Background maps to E, and UniqueShip's background is drawn from the same hydrophones, which avoids the trivial channel-difference cue that an ambient class from another site would create. ShipsEar's own noise class has the opposite problem, as its four noise recordings come from a different place and weather. DeuteroNoise and Oceanship's query vocabulary cover motorboats, sailboats and workboats, so they are the only candidates with any route to B and A, but neither can be downloaded and verified yet. QiandaoEar22 maps at best speedboat to B and tour boat to C, with no A or D.

The coarse mapping that survives scrutiny for a cross-source test is therefore small: vessel present versus background, and perhaps passenger (ShipsEar C plus D) versus other. That is consistent with the PRD's existing P2 wording that the result is an independent-source test of the method, with retraining on each source, not zero-shot transfer. I would not claim a five-class transfer.

## Download size and compute are manageable at 26 GB and risky at 92 GB

At 100 Mbit/s a **26 GB** subset is roughly 35-40 minutes of transfer and the **92 GB** subset is about 2 hours; at 50 Mbit/s the figures double (my arithmetic, ignoring Google Drive throttling, which I did not test). The download comes as 10 GB chunks and the authors recommend rclone ([uniqueshipdata.org](https://uniqueshipdata.org)). The 92 GB figure is the unzipped size, so budget for both chunks and extracted files on disk, which is on the order of 150-200 GB of free space; I did not check whether the zips are compressed enough to change this. The full set (656 GB in .wv, 1.4 TB in .wav) is out of the question. For a compact PyTorch CNN, the working set is features, not audio. If the 12-class subset is 12 classes of 5 h each, that is about 60 h, or about 43,000 5-second clips (my inference from the site's description, not a stated count). Cached log-mel features for that many clips at 16 kHz and 64 mel bins would be around 2 GB, which is manageable on a laptop. The 5-class 92 GB set has more audio, but its hours are not stated, so use the 26 GB set first and filter to the four vessel types plus background. The sampling rates differ across sources (20 kHz for UniqueShip, 32 kHz for DeepShip, 52,734 Hz for ShipsEar, 48 kHz for DeuteroNoise), so the PRD's single resampling rate in D2 should be 16 kHz or lower, which suits all of them. Native 20 kHz audio limits the usable band to 10 kHz.

## Effects on the PRD and the Oct 31 timeline

D1 is the main gain. ShipsEar has no stated licence and access is by emailing the maintainer, while UniqueShip's stated CC-BY 4.0 would permit a manifest-plus-download-script repo and also redistributable sample clips with attribution to ONC, which solves P1-DEMO. One licence caution cuts the other way: the DeuteroNoise paper's comparison table describes ShipsEar as "CC-BY-NC via project website" ([Nou-Plana et al. 2026](https://boa.unimib.it/retrieve/d5750c52-16e3-44ac-a933-2316c2997ca8/Nou-Plana%20et%20al-2026-Applied%20Acoustics-VoR.pdf)), whereas the ShipsEar landing page itself states no licence. That is a secondary claim that contradicts the primary page, so it does not change the working assumption (no redistribution), but the maintainers' reply should settle it.

D2 changes little; the fixed-rate resampler already handles any source, and UniqueShip clips are already 5 s. M3 gets stronger with UniqueShip: with 4,218 MMSIs and vessel-disjoint splits supplied, the zero-shared-vessel assertion tests MMSI, test sets are far larger than ShipsEar's class D of about 9 files, and the repeated grouped-CV workaround for small classes becomes optional. One caveat is that the published splits are 5 s segments, so "recording" in the M3 test means MMSI, and the check that each clip carries MMSI in the actual download has not been done. Scope classes would change if UniqueShip became primary: the closed set becomes Tug/Tow, Tanker, Passenger, Cargo and Background, the PRD's A-E scope sentence and the G1 wording must be rewritten, and the claim becomes "unseen vessels at one ONC site", not "unseen recordings from ShipsEar". P2 improves: UniqueShip is a better-documented second source than DeepShip, with a licence statement, MMSI grouping and no email step, and the PRD's "DeepShip is the leading candidate" line should be replaced. The Multi-dataset training non-goal stays intact if the two are trained and reported separately.

On the timeline, the existing PRD gate of Oct 10 for the ShipsEar email still holds, but it contains a gap: "no reply is pre-accepted as the no-audio fallback" protects the licence story, not the data supply. With no reply, the repo has only the four-file 83.9 MB sample zip and cannot train anything ([ShipsEar landing page](https://underwaternoise.atlanttic.uvigo.es/)). The fix is a decision date, Oct 14, by which either the full ShipsEar files have arrived or the primary becomes UniqueShip. Starting the 26 GB download in Week 1 costs a few hours of transfer, not engineering time, and keeps Week 2 (CNN) and Week 3 (app) intact. Switching on Oct 14 leaves three weeks, which is enough for a pipeline that already treats the dataset loader as a module (PRD audience section). A merged ShipsEar-plus-UniqueShip training run is not advised, since the label sets do not align and the PRD excludes it.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which dataset replaces ShipsEar" to "what protects the project if ShipsEar cannot be fetched." No candidate matches ShipsEar's A-E taxonomy, and the closest in spirit, DeuteroNoise, is unreleased, so the realistic choice is between ShipsEar's richer small-craft classes with uncertain access and UniqueShip's stronger rigor and licence with a different, merchant-heavy label set. That reframing makes the Oct 14 decision date, and not a better dataset, the most valuable addition to the PRD.

Two uncertainties remain open and could each flip the recommendation: whether UniqueShip's CC-BY statement and Google Drive links survive a direct check, and whether the Vigo maintainers answer. If UniqueShip checks out and the maintainers stay silent, the project will have a stronger evaluation story on a different task, and the README should say so plainly.
